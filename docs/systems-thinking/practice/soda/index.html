Systemic intervention | about 40 minutes | free

SODA: build a means-ends map without erasing disagreement

Turn supplied statements into contrasting constructs and a traceable means-ends map.

Framework link: Strategic Options Development and Analysis (SODA). Apprenticeship practice links: K2, K3, S3, S5, S6, S7.

What this exercise can and cannot establish

A basic cognitive-mapping rehearsal. It is not a full negotiated SODA intervention and does not turn participants' causal beliefs into established facts.

This is original learning material, not a reproduced official course. Mechanical checks have determinate answers within the stated case. Worked comparisons for open judgements show defensible reasoning, not the only possible model. Specialist pedagogical review is not recorded.

The case

Fictional case: a service leader says 'Give advisers more discretion so people receive help without repeated approval.' A finance lead says 'Keep approval for unusual spending so the service remains within its budget.' An adviser says 'Waiting for approval means people call again, which leaves less time to resolve cases.' A resident says 'I need a clear answer, not another referral.' None has agreed that eliminating all approval is desirable.

Work through it

Use paper, your own drawing tool or the notes below. Attempt each step before opening its comparison. Describe a diagram in words when that is more accessible.

  1. 1. Construct four short concepts with meaningful contrasts.

    Produce: Four bipolar or contrast-explicit concepts, retaining the speaker's meaning.

    Compare your work for step 1

    Examples: greater bounded adviser discretion / repeated approval; help resolved / further referral; budget kept within agreed limits / uncontrolled spending; clear answer / uncertain next step. The contrasts should clarify this case, not mechanically negate a word. Mark these as provisional formulations to check with speakers.

  2. 2. Connect means to ends and retain the tension.

    Produce: A directed map plus a statement explaining every arrow.

    Compare your work for step 2

    One proposed chain is bounded discretion -> fewer approval waits -> fewer repeat calls -> more time for resolution. Another is appropriate spending control -> budget viability. A tension appears if the same control creates unnecessary waits. Keep the finance concern visible rather than deleting it as anti-change. These links represent supplied or inferred reasoning, with inference labelled.

  3. 3. Find a candidate option that connects the concerns.

    Produce: One option and a question for negotiation.

    Compare your work for step 3

    Delegated spending within agreed limits, with rapid exception handling and review, could support resolution and budget control. Ask the parties what limits, information and review would make it workable. The analyst has generated an option, not obtained agreement.

Check the basic distinctions

These checks test the supplied case, not your overall competence. Open answers and model variants still need your judgement.

1. Which statement is supported by the case?

Answer and reasoning 1

Some approvals may contribute to repeat demand while others protect a valued constraint

The case supports a tension to investigate, not universal agreement or an attribution of bad intent.

2. What is the status of an arrow derived from an interview statement?

Answer and reasoning 2

A causal belief to explore and test

The map preserves reasoning so it can be compared, challenged and revised. It does not make an assertion true merely by drawing it.

Repair a defective model or claim

The facilitator merges 'financial control' and 'bureaucracy' into one negative concept and reports consensus.
Compare your repair

Recover the different meanings and the legitimate concern each expresses. Preserve tensions and return formulations to participants. Agreement must be negotiated, not created through the analyst's labels.

Try a changed case before looking

The adviser says most delays occur after approval, while waiting for a specialist. What changes?

Compare the changed case

Revise the causal chain. Approval may not be the dominant source of delay. Add specialist availability or routing where evidence supports it, and reconsider whether delegated spending addresses the important blockage.

Review the process, not just the score

  • Concepts preserve meaning through useful contrasts.
  • Arrows form explainable means-ends reasoning.
  • Disagreement and inferred claims remain visible.
  • An option is distinguished from negotiated agreement.

A different model can be defensible when its purpose, assumptions and reasoning are explicit. A contradiction, incorrect unit, invented fact or unacknowledged change of purpose needs repair. Keep both your first attempt and revision.
