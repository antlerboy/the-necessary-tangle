Systemic intervention | about 40 minutes | free

Ethics and power: make an intervention's authority and effects explicit

Distinguish permission, participation and legitimacy, then design a reversible intervention with an accessible challenge route.

Framework link: Multi-agency intervention; Values mapping and integration; Managing deflection / resistance & challenge. Apprenticeship practice links: K4, K3, S5, S7, S8, S10, B2, B4, B7.

What this exercise can and cannot establish

A generic ethical reasoning exercise. It cannot establish compliance with sector-specific law, safeguarding or professional requirements. Those require qualified, contextual checks before real action.

This is original learning material, not a reproduced official course. Mechanical checks have determinate answers within the stated case. Worked comparisons for open judgements show defensible reasoning, not the only possible model. Specialist pedagogical review is not recorded.

The case

Fictional case: a sponsor asks an analyst to rank families for extra support using attendance records. The sponsor controls the project budget but does not control the schools' data. Families have not been told about the proposed use. The analyst suspects that absence may reflect transport barriers, caring responsibilities or illness, but no inquiry has tested those explanations. The sponsor says consultation will slow delivery and asks for a hidden pilot.

Work through it

Use paper, your own drawing tool or the notes below. Attempt each step before opening its comparison. Describe a diagram in words when that is more accessible.

  1. 1. Map authority and affectedness.

    Produce: A distinction between budget authority, data rights and effects on people.

    Compare your work for step 1

    The sponsor's budget authority does not automatically authorise access to school data or a new use of it. Families are affected even if they do not control the project. Identify the actual accountable organisations and required data, professional and safeguarding checks rather than inventing legal permission.

  2. 2. Separate observations, labels and causal claims.

    Produce: Three typed statements and alternative explanations.

    Compare your work for step 2

    Recorded attendance is an observation with possible data-quality limits. 'Disengaged family' is an interpretation. 'Low attendance means low motivation' is a causal claim not established here. Transport, caring and illness are competing hypotheses, not facts about any family.

  3. 3. Design an ethically defensible next inquiry.

    Produce: A narrower action with permission, safeguards and a stop condition.

    Compare your work for step 3

    First clarify lawful authority and purpose with the relevant professionals. Seek a proportionate, transparent inquiry that includes affected families and does not determine support from an untested proxy alone. Use minimised or synthetic data for the modelling rehearsal. Establish how people can correct information or challenge an interpretation. A hidden pilot is not made acceptable by being small.

  4. 4. Prepare a constructive challenge to the sponsor.

    Produce: A short statement that names the risk and a workable alternative.

    Compare your work for step 4

    For example: 'We can test the model with synthetic cases now. Using identifiable attendance to rank families needs authority and a way to test the proxy with affected people. Otherwise the model may direct support away from the people facing the largest barriers.' Keep the challenge specific; do not accuse the sponsor of a motive the evidence does not establish.

Check the basic distinctions

These checks test the supplied case, not your overall competence. Open answers and model variants still need your judgement.

1. Does budget ownership automatically authorise the proposed data use?

Answer and reasoning 1

No

Different authorities and obligations may apply. This exercise provides no sector-specific legal determination.

2. What is the status of 'absence means low motivation'?

Answer and reasoning 2

An untested causal interpretation

The same observation can arise from different conditions. A consequential proxy requires evidence and challenge.

Repair a defective model or claim

The ethical review says 'all stakeholders consulted' because the sponsor approved the model.
Compare your repair

Name who actually participated, who was affected but absent, what could be changed through participation, and who had decision rights. Approval and legitimacy are not interchangeable.

Try a changed case before looking

Families agree to discuss barriers but decline automated ranking. What follows?

Compare the changed case

Consent to one activity does not imply consent or authorisation for another. Redesign the inquiry and decision process within applicable obligations. Their refusal is evidence about the intervention's acceptability, not a modelling error to overcome.

Review the process, not just the score

  • Authority, influence, affectedness and permission are distinct.
  • Proxies and causal claims are not treated as established truth.
  • The intervention has transparency, safeguards and a challenge route.
  • Sector-specific compliance is explicitly left for qualified checking.

A different model can be defensible when its purpose, assumptions and reasoning are explicit. A contradiction, incorrect unit, invented fact or unacknowledged change of purpose needs repair. Keep both your first attempt and revision.
