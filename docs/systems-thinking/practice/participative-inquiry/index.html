Systemic intervention | about 40 minutes | free

Participative inquiry: test who your evidence leaves out

Build an engagement and evidence plan that does not confuse influence with the right to be heard.

Framework link: Stakeholder analysis; Influence mapping; Interview technique; Listening & multiple perspectives; Large group engagement processes. Apprenticeship practice links: K3, K4, S5, S6, S10, B2, B4, B8, B9.

What this exercise can and cannot establish

A basic inquiry-design rehearsal. An invented engagement plan is not evidence of actual facilitation, safe participation or sustained relationships.

This is original learning material, not a reproduced official course. Mechanical checks have determinate answers within the stated case. Worked comparisons for open judgements show defensible reasoning, not the only possible model. Specialist pedagogical review is not recorded.

The case

Fictional case: a transport partnership surveys people through its smartphone ticketing app. Managers report that 92% of respondents are satisfied. The response count and invitation count are not given. Drivers say some passengers pay cash and need help. A disability group cannot attend the partnership's daytime meetings. The operator controls timetables, the council controls a subsidy, and passengers have informal influence but no direct timetable authority.

Work through it

Use paper, your own drawing tool or the notes below. Attempt each step before opening its comparison. Describe a diagram in words when that is more accessible.

  1. 1. Map interest, influence, control and affectedness separately.

    Produce: A four-column actor table.

    Compare your work for step 1

    The operator controls timetable decisions; the council controls subsidy; drivers hold operational knowledge; passengers experience the service; the disability group can supply perspectives absent from the meetings. Low formal power does not mean low relevance. Distinguish resource control from expertise and affectedness.

  2. 2. Audit the 92% statement.

    Produce: Three missing facts and one excluded group.

    Compare your work for step 2

    Ask how many were invited, how many responded, what question was asked, when, and whether repeat responses were possible. People without the app are outside the sampling frame. The percentage may describe respondents accurately while saying little about all passengers or people unable to travel.

  3. 3. Design a proportionate mixed inquiry.

    Produce: Two evidence routes and an accessible participation option.

    Compare your work for step 3

    Observe a small range of journeys and interview cash and app users with consent. Hold supported conversations at accessible times or through an independent group. Retain anonymous reporting where useful. Ask an open question such as 'Tell me about the last journey you tried to make', then check interpretation with the speaker.

  4. 4. Plan a group comparison without forced consensus.

    Produce: An opening question, a participation safeguard and a record format.

    Compare your work for step 4

    Ask 'What makes a journey usable, and for whom does the current arrangement fail?' Give participants different ways to contribute and separate operator authority from facilitation. Record agreements, differences, missing evidence and commitments. A World Cafe or other format is a means, not proof that all relevant voices are present.

Check the basic distinctions

These checks test the supplied case, not your overall competence. Open answers and model variants still need your judgement.

1. Can 92% app-user satisfaction establish satisfaction among all potential passengers?

Answer and reasoning 1

No: the sampling boundary excludes some relevant people

The frame, response process and question matter. An accurate percentage within one group does not automatically generalise.

2. Which interview opening best avoids importing a diagnosis?

Answer and reasoning 2

Tell me about the last journey you tried to make

It invites an account before assigning a motive or preferred judgement.

Repair a defective model or claim

Only high-power stakeholders are invited because low-power passengers cannot affect delivery.
Compare your repair

Separate ability to block a decision from entitlement to contribute evidence and challenge its effects. Design routes for affected people to influence the inquiry and explain what authority remains elsewhere.

Try a changed case before looking

The disability group declines the proposed workshop. What should not be inferred, and what is a better next move?

Compare the changed case

Do not infer indifference or consent. Ask whether timing, trust, access, burden or previous experience is the issue, and offer a less burdensome route through their preferred representative or format.

Review the process, not just the score

  • Influence and affectedness are not conflated.
  • Sampling limits are explicit.
  • Questions seek accounts before explanations.
  • Participation has a practical route to affect the inquiry.

A different model can be defensible when its purpose, assumptions and reasoning are explicit. A contradiction, incorrect unit, invented fact or unacknowledged change of purpose needs repair. Keep both your first attempt and revision.
