Fractal Enterprise Model: alternate processes and the assets they depend on

Build a small process-asset model and identify a neglected asset-management dependency.

Framework link: Fractal Enterprise Model & Capabilities (Bider). Apprenticeship practice links: K2, S2, S3, S4, S7.

What this exercise can and cannot establish

A first FEM modelling exercise using explicit text relations. Follow the linked reference guides for the complete notation and allowed label combinations.

This is original learning material, not a reproduced official course. Mechanical checks have determinate answers within the stated case. Worked comparisons for open judgements show defensible reasoning, not the only possible model. Specialist pedagogical review is not recorded.

The case

Fictional case: an advice charity delivers appointments. This process uses trained advisers, an appointment system and suitable rooms. Advisers are maintained through supervision and training; new advisers come through recruitment. The appointment system is maintained by a supplier-management process. Rooms depend on facilities maintenance. Every supporting process also needs people, information and time. Recruitment was suspended because managers counted only time spent on appointments as productive.

Work through it

Use paper, your own drawing tool or the notes below. Attempt each step before opening its comparison. Describe a diagram in words when that is more accessible.

  1. 1. Separate processes from assets.

    Produce: Two lists and a notation legend.

    Compare your work for step 1

    Processes include delivering appointments, training advisers, recruitment, supplier management and facilities maintenance. Assets include the available adviser capability, appointment system and rooms. In the introductory FEM notation use ovals for processes and rectangles for assets, or labelled text equivalents. 'Recruitment' is not interchangeable with 'trained advisers': one changes an asset the other process needs.

  2. 2. Construct one complete alternating chain.

    Produce: A process-asset-process-asset chain with typed relations.

    Compare your work for step 2

    Deliver appointments uses adviser capability; training maintains that capability; training itself uses trainers, time and learning material. Recruitment acquires new adviser capacity. Supplier management maintains access to the appointment system. Specify the relation in words rather than drawing an unlabelled arrow whose direction could mean several things.

  3. 3. Use the model to challenge the productivity decision.

    Produce: A dependency-based prediction and an observation plan.

    Compare your work for step 3

    Suspending recruitment can weaken future adviser capacity even while today's appointment utilisation rises. Track departures, recruitment lead time, capability and available appointments. The model identifies a dependency; it does not calculate the rate of decline without further data or a dynamic model.

Check the basic distinctions

These checks test the supplied case, not your overall competence. Open answers and model variants still need your judgement.

1. Which is an asset in this case?

Answer and reasoning 1

Available trained adviser capability

The capability is something processes use and maintain. Recruitment and supervision are activities that affect it.

2. Does drawing the dependency predict the exact staffing shortage next year?

Answer and reasoning 2

No, the dependency model needs further quantitative assumptions for that prediction

A structural relation is not a calibrated time-dependent model.

Repair a defective model or claim

A chronological flowchart of appointment steps is labelled FEM, with every item drawn as the same type of box.
Compare your repair

Distinguish processes, assets and the relations of use and asset management. A process flowchart may be useful, but it answers a different question.

Try a changed case before looking

The charity outsources the appointment system. Does that remove it from the model?

Compare the changed case

No. The capability remains a dependency even when externally provided. Model the asset or access it supplies, the supplier relationship that sustains it, and the relevant boundary and control limits.

Review the process, not just the score

  • Processes and assets are distinct.
  • Use and maintenance/acquisition relations are labelled.
  • At least one supporting process's own dependencies are exposed.
  • Structural insight is not passed off as a quantitative forecast.

A different model can be defensible when its purpose, assumptions and reasoning are explicit. A contradiction, incorrect unit, invented fact or unacknowledged change of purpose needs repair. Keep both your first attempt and revision.
