Socio-Technical Systems: redesign the work and technology together

Find where variation is handled and design a jointly workable social and technical arrangement.

Framework link: Socio-Technical Systems (Trist). Apprenticeship practice links: K2, K3, S3, S5, S7, S8.

What this exercise can and cannot establish

A basic primary-work-system exercise informed by the socio-technical tradition. It is not a complete participative design process or a substitute for workers' and users' involvement.

This is original learning material, not a reproduced official course. Mechanical checks have determinate answers within the stated case. Worked comparisons for open judgements show defensible reasoning, not the only possible model. Specialist pedagogical review is not recorded.

The case

Fictional case: a repair service introduces handheld job software. The software permits only one fault code per visit. Workers often find several linked faults, but cannot alter the code or order additional parts. A supervisor can approve changes the next morning. Residents then receive extra appointments. Management proposes training workers to comply more consistently. Workers propose discretion to record linked faults, while the safety lead insists that electrical work remains restricted to qualified staff.

Work through it

Use paper, your own drawing tool or the notes below. Attempt each step before opening its comparison. Describe a diagram in words when that is more accessible.

  1. 1. Define the primary task and the social and technical arrangements.

    Produce: A short work-system account rather than a technology inventory.

    Compare your work for step 1

    Primary task: complete agreed safe repairs in residents' homes. Technical arrangements include fault coding, devices, records and parts ordering. Social arrangements include skills, authority, division of work, supervision and resident communication. These interact: a software field can constrain discretion, and an authority rule can turn a technically possible update into a day's delay.

  2. 2. Locate a significant variance and where it is currently controlled.

    Produce: A variance-control trace.

    Compare your work for step 2

    The observed condition differs from the single coded fault. The worker encounters the variation at the home, but control sits with a next-day supervisor and a rigid data structure. Trace the resulting hand-off, delay and repeat visit. Do not assume all variation should be eliminated; some reflects the actual repair need.

  3. 3. Design a joint change with minimum necessary constraints.

    Produce: A paired social/technical proposal with a safety boundary.

    Compare your work for step 3

    Allow linked faults to be recorded and parts requested within defined competence and spending limits. Give suitably trained workers the authority to use that capability. Preserve qualified electrical work and a rapid escalation route for unsafe or unfamiliar conditions. A new app without authority, or autonomy without information and competence, is incomplete.

  4. 4. Test the whole work system.

    Produce: Three measures and a participative review question.

    Compare your work for step 4

    Inspect safe completed repairs, resident disruption and worker ability to resolve variation, alongside resource use. Review cases jointly with workers and residents. Faster data entry alone may coexist with more repeat visits. Ask whose work or risk has been moved elsewhere by the redesign.

Check the basic distinctions

These checks test the supplied case, not your overall competence. Open answers and model variants still need your judgement.

1. Which proposal addresses both social and technical design?

Answer and reasoning 1

Enable linked-fault recording and give qualified workers bounded authority to act

Usable capability depends on the record, skills, rights and work arrangements together.

2. Where is the variance first encountered?

Answer and reasoning 2

At the home by the worker

The mismatch between the coded job and actual faults is encountered in the primary work. The question is how safely and effectively it can be handled there.

3. Does local discretion require removing electrical qualification rules?

Answer and reasoning 3

No: discretion can operate within necessary competence and safety constraints

Joint optimisation is not unlimited autonomy. Necessary constraints and accessible support are part of the design.

Repair a defective model or claim

The project buys the software first and calls a final staff briefing 'participative socio-technical design'.
Compare your repair

Involve those doing and receiving the work while task, technology, authority and boundaries are still open to change. Evaluate the combined arrangement, not attendance at the briefing.

Try a changed case before looking

Workers can now edit codes, but the parts store still accepts orders only from supervisors. What remains?

Compare the changed case

The technical edit has not removed the control bottleneck. Examine ordering authority, stock information and exceptions as part of the same work system. A local software improvement may simply relocate the delay.

Review the process, not just the score

  • The primary task and social/technical interdependence are explicit.
  • Variation is traced through actual work and control locations.
  • Authority, competence, information and technology change coherently.
  • Safety and effects on residents and other workers remain visible.

A different model can be defensible when its purpose, assumptions and reasoning are explicit. A contradiction, incorrect unit, invented fact or unacknowledged change of purpose needs repair. Keep both your first attempt and revision.
