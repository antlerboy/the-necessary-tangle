Systemic intervention | about 50 minutes | free

Critical social learning: build a learning relationship across boundaries

Design a small collective inquiry that can change both action and the assumptions behind it.

Framework link: Critical Social Learning Systems; Action learning; Action research; Appreciative Enquiry. Apprenticeship practice links: K3, K4, S5, S6, S7, S8, S9, S11.

What this exercise can and cannot establish

A bridge exercise across related but distinct approaches. Vickers' appreciative systems are not identical to Cooperrider's 4D Appreciative Inquiry. Systems convening offers practical social-learning resources, not a claim to be the same named methodology.

This is original learning material, not a reproduced official course. Mechanical checks have determinate answers within the stated case. Worked comparisons for open judgements show defensible reasoning, not the only possible model. Specialist pedagogical review is not recorded.

The case

Fictional case: a council, a charity and a residents' group meet monthly about access to advice. Each presents success figures, but nobody changes practice. The council values prompt closure, the charity values sustained relationships, and residents value getting an answer they can use. One joint case worked unusually well because a resident and adviser could contact a named decision-maker directly. That person has since left. Residents say meetings use unfamiliar language and are held during their working hours.

Work through it

Use paper, your own drawing tool or the notes below. Attempt each step before opening its comparison. Describe a diagram in words when that is more accessible.

  1. 1. Map the appreciative differences.

    Produce: Three judgements about what counts as a good result, without forcing one metric.

    Compare your work for step 1

    Prompt closure, sustained relationship and a usable answer are different appreciations of the situation. Ask what experiences and standards sustain each judgement. This is an appreciative-systems inquiry in Vickers' sense; it is not simply a request to think positively.

  2. 2. Investigate the successful case without ignoring failure.

    Produce: Evidence of enabling conditions and a question about transfer.

    Compare your work for step 2

    Ask what the named decision-maker could actually do, what trust and access made possible, and whether the arrangement depended on unusual unpaid effort. A 4D Appreciative Inquiry route could explore successful experience, imagine a better arrangement, design it and sustain learning; keep that distinct from the earlier appreciative-systems analysis. Do not assume a single success can be copied unchanged.

  3. 3. Design an action-research cycle.

    Produce: A jointly owned question, small action, observation and reflection.

    Compare your work for step 3

    Question: can a named decision route improve usable answers without creating an unmanageable dependency? Agree a small trial with participants, gather resident accounts and response traces, and review together. Change the meeting time and language so residents can participate. Record who can stop or revise the trial.

  4. 4. Create a double-loop learning test.

    Produce: One change to action and one change to a governing assumption.

    Compare your work for step 4

    Changing how quickly cases are closed is an action adjustment. Questioning whether closure is an adequate definition of success changes a governing assumption. The group should show what it learned and altered, not merely that it held another meeting.

Check the basic distinctions

These checks test the supplied case, not your overall competence. Open answers and model variants still need your judgement.

1. Does a positive case remove the need to investigate exclusions?

Answer and reasoning 1

No, enabling conditions and excluded experiences both matter

Appreciation is not a licence to omit harms or transfer conditions.

2. Which is the clearest double-loop move?

Answer and reasoning 2

Question whether closure represents the purpose people value

It examines the rule or assumption shaping action, rather than only improving performance within it.

Repair a defective model or claim

The group records attendance as proof that a social learning system exists and that residents share the official purpose.
Compare your repair

Look for changes in shared inquiry, relationships, action and the capacity to challenge assumptions. Attendance alone establishes none of those. Ask residents whether the process is usable and consequential.

Try a changed case before looking

The named decision route improves speed but residents feel pressured to accept an answer. What should the learning group do?

Compare the changed case

Revise the definition of success to include the quality and freedom of the interaction. Investigate the pressure, change the process with residents and reconsider the trial's limits. Do not treat the complaint as resistance to an otherwise successful innovation.

Review the process, not just the score

  • Different appreciations and named traditions are not collapsed.
  • Participation changes who can influence the inquiry.
  • The action cycle includes evidence and revision.
  • A governing assumption can be questioned.

A different model can be defensible when its purpose, assumptions and reasoning are explicit. A contradiction, incorrect unit, invented fact or unacknowledged change of purpose needs repair. Keep both your first attempt and revision.
