Test the claim

A plausible systems claim can survive a workshop and still fail a simple question. Work through a short fictional case, then test your answer against a counterexample. The point is to find the next useful observation or decision.

Allow about 15 minutes per case. Write your answer before opening the comparison. These are editorial learning routes, not new relationships in the evidence graph, an assessment, or a substitute for work with people in the situation.

Eight claims to test

Our booking service is performing better: it allocated 120 appointments instead of 100.

Boundary comparison

The queue has shrunk. Has the work?

We closed 80 cases this month, so the backlog must have fallen by 80.

Accounting check followed by evaluation

More options, more capability?

We need a separate procedure for every kind of request: requisite variety demands it.

Regulation question followed by organisational diagnosis

A job title is not a function

The chief executive is System 5, so we have found the organisation's policy function.

Functional diagnosis checked against the chosen boundary

Everyone was consulted

The workshop was representative because every stakeholder group received an invitation.

Boundary critique followed by inquiry design

Four methods, one answer?

We used four systems methods, so the recommendation has four times the support.
