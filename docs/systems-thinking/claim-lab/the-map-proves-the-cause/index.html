The map proves the cause

Everybody recognised the feedback loop, so it must explain the problem.

The case

A fictional library team draws a loop: less assistance leads to incomplete applications, which lead to repeat visits, which consume assistance time. Staff recognise the story. No repeat-visit data have been collected. A software failure and a change in eligibility rules occurred during the same period.

Try it before reading on

Turn the loop into a bounded hypothesis. Specify one observation that would challenge it and one competing explanation worth investigating.

Use paper or your own notes. Keep the first answer as well as any revision.

Compare your reasoning

Recognition makes the hypothesis worth examining; it does not establish the mechanism. State which visitors, period, and assistance changes the claim covers. Examine whether incomplete applications actually precede repeat visits and whether repeats consume the relevant capacity. A decline in repeats despite reduced assistance would challenge the proposed loop, although changed access could complicate interpretation. Investigate the software and eligibility changes rather than crediting one familiar story with every effect.

When could the claim be defensible?

Shared practitioner recognition can be useful evidence of experience. It becomes stronger when specific observations and contradictory cases can be inspected. Dismissing it merely because it is qualitative would lose relevant information.

Change the conditions

Your new reporting makes staff record repeat visits more carefully. Recorded repeats rise. What belongs in the observer note?

Compare the changed case

The measurement intervention changed recording behaviour, so a rise may partly reflect ascertainment. Explain your role, retain the definition change, and seek comparable evidence before interpreting it as a worsening service.
