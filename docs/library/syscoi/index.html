Discovering network resilience formulas with symbolized reinforcement learning

Yu Zheng, Jingtao Ding, Depeng Jin, Jianxi Gao, Yong Li

Yu Zheng and colleagues report a method that turns learned network-attack strategies into interpretable formulas for assessing resilience. Their account considers both network structure and the dynamical states imposed on it, including the association between node degree and state. The method aims to identify components whose removal can cause a network to fail. The Nature Communications abstract is the basis of this description: the reported performance improvements belong to the authors’ evaluated settings, and do not establish a general measure for every social or organisational use of resilience.

Second Law Studio – The Right to Stop

Derek Smith, Second Law Studio

Second Law Studio’s first research paper, The Right to Stop, examines Lean practices through the authority to raise evidence and change the operating model. The publisher describes unresolved evidence as epistemic work-in-progress, asking how it is noticed, admitted, assigned, and resolved when correction capacity is overloaded. It proposes operational corrigibility as a capacity for repeated correction. This description is based on the studio’s own public announcement; the paper’s full argument was not independently assessed here.

Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction

Bret Victor

Bret Victor’s October 2011 interactive essay uses a simple car-control simulation to show how designers can move between a particular run and broader views of possible behaviour. Time and steering parameters can be varied; trajectories expose patterns that are difficult to see during real-time playback. Moving back to a concrete case tests what an abstract picture hides. The essay offers a practical approach to exploring a system when theory is insufficient to predict the consequences of design decisions.

Description based on A Systematic Approach to Interactive Visualization; Controlling Time; Abstracting Over Time; Stepping Back Down (checked 2026-11-07).

SysCoI post, 2026-11-02 by Benjamin P Taylor

Chaos to coordination: vibrating material organises itself into a single rhythm – University of Birmingham

University of Birmingham

The University of Birmingham reports experiments in which active mechanical units settle into coordinated oscillation through local, non-reciprocal interactions. Initially short waves grow in scale, period, and amplitude until the material moves together. The report describes both a honeycomb assembly and an elastic ring, and calls the process wave coarsening. It provides a concrete physical example of coordination arising from designed interactions. Proposed uses in robotics remain prospective applications of the reported experiments.

Description based on 14 October 2026 report; non-reciprocal interactions; 2D honeycomb material (checked 2026-11-07).

Richard S O’Rourke

Richard S O’Rourke

Richard S O’Rourke sets out a research programme on sustainability that brings energy and material flows into conversation with cybernetic regulation and political economy. His homepage distinguishes thermodynamic entropy from information entropy, and follows a route through Lotka, Ashby, and economic theory. Its research list separates working drafts from published work. This is the same author’s research hub already represented in the atlas; the later SysCoI post also supplies his Substack writing route.
