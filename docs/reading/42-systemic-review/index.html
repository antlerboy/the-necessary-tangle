42 The Systemic Review: examining the diagnosis

42 The Systemic Review is SCiO’s publication and policy-engagement programme. Its About page describes a way to examine a public-policy problem through selected systems laws, principles, and ways of thinking. The publication says it makes evidence, interpretation, boundaries, and assumptions visible. Those commitments can be tested against each paper.

Read a diagnosis as an argument

  1. Check what the selected system includes and what it leaves outside.
  2. Follow the factual claims back to the cited evidence.
  3. Identify the mechanism linking the stated principle to the observed pattern.
  4. Ask what observation would weaken that explanation.
  5. Consider the effects on people outside the diagnosis and who could change the relevant conditions.

These are editorial reading questions. Naming a principle does not establish that its application fits the case. Agreement between two methods may help expose an explanation, but shared assumptions can also make them repeat the same error.

Keep the scope visible

The publication’s name refers to its chosen set of 42 laws, principles, and ways of thinking, with a Douglas Adams reference. This guide does not treat that selection as a universally agreed canon. Its account of a rapid, usually free initial review is a statement of the programme’s offer; consult its current contact page about a specific proposal.
