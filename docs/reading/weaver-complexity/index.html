Organisational application: what would you need to add?

This is an original editorial exercise, not a claim that Weaver investigated this service or endorsed the proposed method.

A fictional housing team proposes to predict demand using a single mean arrival rate. Staff report coordinated surges after letters are issued, and repeat contacts after failed repairs. The sponsor also wants the model to decide who should receive a scarce appointment first.

  1. Specify the decision the prediction must support. What timing, grouping, and repeated contacts does the average conceal?
  2. State a test under which the proposed model would be useful, and a pattern that would expose its limits. What additional data would discriminate between those possibilities?
  3. Choose who must work together on this question and what each contributes. Explain which work can sensibly be done independently.
  4. Separate a prediction about consequences from a judgement about fair access. Who has authority to make that judgement, and how can affected people challenge it?
Compare a possible response

Track arrivals against the letter schedule and retain the link between a failed repair and a repeat contact. Compare the simple average with observed peaks before adopting a more elaborate model. Bring service knowledge and analytical skills together for the decisions that need both. A technically credible forecast still leaves the allocation criteria and legitimate authority to be settled. Neither a historical citation nor the model grants that authority.
