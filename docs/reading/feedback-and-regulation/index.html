Feedback and regulation: trace the mechanism

A returned signal tells us about a relation. To judge regulation, we also need to know what can act, what is protected, and which outcomes count as acceptable.

Separate the questions

Questions that require different evidence
QuestionWhat has to be specified?
Is there feedback?Which parts affect which others, what returns, and what definition is being used?
What is its sign?The local relation or loop, the reference condition, and the relevant operating or frequency conditions.
Is the response stable?The complete dynamics and the precise stability question; a correction sign alone is insufficient.
Is anything usefully regulated?Essential variables, acceptable states, disturbances, the available responses, and evidence of what actually happens.

These distinctions organise this inquiry. They do not force all three historical sources into one vocabulary or make this finite example a general theory of organisations.

What the selected primary passages establish

F1 · located primary

James Clerk Maxwell

Maxwell separates decaying from growing disturbances, including oscillatory cases. He describes governor adjustment becoming too strong for stable motion. His continuous mechanical analysis does not establish the thresholds of the discrete example below.

F2 · located primary

Arturo Rosenblueth, Norbert Wiener, and Julian Bigelow

The authors acknowledge a broader return-of-output usage, then restrict feedback to error-controlled negative feedback. Their overshoot account says inadequate damping can make feedback positive at particular frequencies. Preserve that qualification.

pp19–20; PDF3–4

F3 · located primary

W. Ross Ashby

Ashby uses feedback for mutual effects, discusses a narrower return-connection usage, and warns that circuit accounts alone do not determine the behaviour of richly interconnected wholes.

§4/11, pp53–54; PDF31–32

W. Ross Ashby

Ashby specifies essential variables and acceptable states; protection may be passive or active. He distinguishes response to a threatened disturbance from error-based correction. His acceptable set is given outside the analysis; useful partial regulation can permit small errors.

Try an opposing correction that can still overshoot

In this original discrete example, for k>0, each nonzero correction opposes the current displacement from a fixed target. Change the gain and compare the analytic behaviour with the observed tolerance band. The calculation is stipulated; it has no measured service or organisational parameters.

The correction’s derivative with respect to the current state is −k. The multiplier of successive errors is 1−k. Confusing those two signs hides the very behaviour the example exposes. The model contains an integrating state update, with no additional delay, plant dynamics, noise, saturation, or continuing disturbance. A varying target changes the recurrence.

Maxwell’s mechanical governors have continuous dynamics; the unit-step gain thresholds here are not his result. Nor does Ashby’s formulation-specific argument about perfect error-controlled regulation forbid every exact one-step correction in a different model. Our k=1 example has a fixed target, exact measurement, no subsequent disturbance, and the stipulated update.

Trace a real mechanism before naming its success

  1. Name the protected variable and range. Who needs it to remain there, who chose the range, and whose interests or conditions are outside that choice?
  2. Follow one disturbance. Record what arrives, what is observed, how it is interpreted, the decision, the available action, and what happens next. Keep missing evidence visible.
  3. Identify the route. Is protection passive, a response to a threatened disturbance, correction after an effect, or some combination? A reporting meeting alone does not establish a working loop.
  4. Check response capacity and consequences. Who can actually act, with what resources and discretion? Compare intended correction with observed outcomes, including delay and unwanted effects.
  5. Test a competing account. What observation would show that the proposed loop or regulatory explanation is wrong? Who can validate or revise the account?

These are new inquiry prompts, not a validated diagnostic instrument or evidence that importing a control diagram improves a public service.

The existing profiles

Feedback

Feedback is not merely receiving comments. It is a circular relation in which consequences return to influence what happens next. The loop may counteract change, reinforce it, oscillate, delay response or fail because no actor can use the returned information. A correction sign, a stable response, and acceptable performance are separate questions. Historical authors also used different boundaries for the term.

Retained canonical definition: A circular causal or informational relation in which an output or consequence affects later input, state or action. The sign, delay, reference condition and mechanism must be stated.

Existing open checks retained

Regulation

Regulation is the work of keeping outcomes within acceptable ranges despite disturbance. It may operate through feedback, anticipation, constraints, buffering, redundancy, local autonomy or changes to the environment. Specify the essential variables, acceptable set, and disturbance conditions. Ashby distinguishes passive protection, response to a threatened disturbance, and error-based correction; an error need not imply that useful regulation has failed.

Retained canonical definition: A relation among disturbances, a reguland, a regulator and acceptable outcomes in which outcome variety is constrained relative to a stated goal or essential-variable range.

Existing open checks retained
  • legal and institutional regulation as a distinct lineage
  • distributed regulation cases
