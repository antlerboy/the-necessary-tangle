OrgLens: models, checks, and organisational judgement

OrgLens turns accounts of an organisation into a report through three systems lenses. Doug Belshaw’s source repository describes an experiment tested on fictional organisations, simulated participants, and AI judges. Its usefulness in a real organisation remains to be established.

OrgLens (formerly VSMkit) · Open the tool · Plain-English guide

Follow the evidence through the pipeline

The technical account separates four operations: a model represents what people said; code checks that representation; fixed wording describes the findings; and a language model writes a report that receives further quotation and consistency checks. A correct quotation establishes that the words were present. It does not establish that the speaker’s account was complete or that the diagnosis follows.

Use the differences between the lenses

The lens-selection survey explains why the project added feedback and perspective checks. Its compressed descriptions of the parent methods also need scrutiny. For example, treating VSM as assuming an uncontested purpose would turn a modelling choice into a property of the method. A finding about an interview representation cannot settle a dispute about an organisation.

Compare Viable System Model (VSM), System Dynamics, Soft Systems Methodology (SSM), and Critical Systems Heuristics (CSH). The tool selects parts of these traditions; it does not replace their broader practice.

Questions for a practitioner

  1. Invite the people described to correct the model and identify voices it missed.
  2. Separate the report’s quotations, inferences, and suggested actions.
  3. Check a proposed causal pattern against events over time and competing explanations.
  4. Find who could authorise the suggested change and what constraints they face.
  5. Record what happened after using the report, including harm, disagreement, and conclusions that failed.

These are editorial questions for using a diagnostic aid, not findings from OrgLens trials.

Evaluation, data, and reuse

The project’s evaluation account reports experiments on three fictional cases. Simulated reader clarity and checked quotations do not demonstrate organisational improvement. Its README also says submitted material goes to third-party model services and asks users to leave out private information; consult the current service before using it.

The repository states GNU AGPL version 3 or later. This guide links and paraphrases; no OrgLens code is included in the atlas. The earlier development article supplies a separate account of the plain-language reporting work.
