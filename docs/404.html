Start with a question. Follow the connections.

The Necessary Tangle shows what ideas mean, where they came from, what they depend on, how they developed, and how people use them in practice.

Every connection must say what it means. A historical precursor is not automatically a logical prerequisite. Citation is not mentorship. Resemblance is not evidence of influence.

What happens next

  1. The proposal becomes a visible GitHub issue.
  2. Existing entries and duplicates are checked.
  3. The precise claim and sources are examined.
  4. The proposal may be accepted, revised, held as disputed or declined.
  5. Accepted changes appear in a numbered release with a public history.

Questions and disagreement are part of the work. You do not need to arrive with a finished answer.

Purpose, method and limits

About The Necessary Tangle

A reference work should explain its judgements without forcing readers to learn its database vocabulary.

What it is

The Necessary Tangle is a navigable, evidence-backed account of systems, complexity and cybernetics: concepts, people, methods, publications, institutions and traditions, and the different ways they are connected.

It is an atlas for readers and a revisable argument about the field. A graph is selective and interpretive; drawing it does not make it neutral.

Why ‘necessary’?

The project asks which earlier ideas are genuinely needed to understand a later one, which adjacent material is needed to explain the field, and what evidence is needed before a connection is treated as established.

Every line makes a claim

A line may represent logical dependence, historical sequence, influence, teaching, collaboration, practical use, comparison or dispute. Select it to see the wording, limits, status and sources. ‘Related to’ is not enough.

Scope

The core is systems, complexity and cybernetics. Adjacent material is included when it materially explains a central idea, practice, lineage or dispute. The aim is not to map all human thought.

Sources and openness

Every link on this website is public. Some books, archive items and published materials have no open web copy; these are cited as ‘No public link’. Private email, company systems and internal working documents are not linked or quoted in the public release.

How to read status

‘Developed entry’ means a fuller research pass, not final consensus. ‘Brief entry’ offers responsible orientation and sources. ‘Outline only’ marks material that still needs a reliable public account. Disputed connections can remain visible as disputed.

Corrections and dialogue

Corrections are proposed through GitHub Issues. Open questions and wider interpretations belong in GitHub Discussions. Accepted changes are versioned; disagreement is recorded rather than erased when the evidence remains contested.

Editorial and technical detail

Different connections answer different questions

Historical sequence, logical dependence, explicit influence, teaching, collaboration, citation and practical use are represented separately. Treating them all as ‘related to’ produces false family trees.

Claims remain inspectable

Each proposed connection has a direction, wording, scope, status and sources. Competing accounts can coexist. Editorial acceptance means that the evidence currently supports the stated claim; it does not make the field finally settled.

What the website publishes

The GitHub Pages build contains only the public dataset and public website. Private research leads and internal working material are excluded from the repository.
