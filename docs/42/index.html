How to play

Type commands or select the suggestions. Start with LOOK, EXAMINE an object, or a direction: NORTH, SOUTH, EAST, WEST (N, S, E, W). HELP lists the vocabulary. HINT offers progressively more explicit help. The ferry needs a compass, lamp, and chart; other places remain open while you look for them.

FIND searches the public atlas. OPEN selects a search result. FOLLOW lists actual recorded relationships; FOLLOW and a number inspects one. READ ATLAS opens a real link in the fieldnotes. IMPROBABILITY chooses from the whole canonical public collection, avoiding repeat discoveries until that pool is exhausted.

Some apparatus changes when you WAIT or operate it. You can investigate freely. SAVE records a manual checkpoint, separately from the automatic resume state. RESTORE returns to it. RESTART YES begins again and preserves the manual checkpoint until your next SAVE. READ LOG recalls your commands. Browser history, ordinary links, and opening an exit in another tab work too.

Keyboard: Enter submits, Up and Down recall your commands, and Tab reaches suggestions, exits, and sources. The display menu offers green, amber, and paper. This site sends no commands, save files, or expedition logs to a server.

About this edition and the original game

This is an original adventure by The Necessary Tangle, curated by Benjamin P Taylor, with AI assistance. It takes its terminal form and spirit of exploration from the CP/M edition of The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy, by Douglas Adams and Steve Meretzky, published by Infocom in 1984. 2026 is forty-two years later.

The original game: authors, history, and play links · Infocom’s original instruction manual. The original game’s text and software are not reproduced here. The display colours are this edition’s choices; CP/M computers used different terminals.

Fictional rooms, objects, voices, and exits belong to the adventure. Atlas records preserve their own wording, evidence, review status, and limits. A fictional neighbour does not establish historical influence. The apparatus contains bounded original models; its technical notes say what each computes.
