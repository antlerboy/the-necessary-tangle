How to use The Necessary Tangle

You can use The Necessary Tangle to look up an idea, discover its context, or compare accounts of it. Start with something you want to understand; the complete map can wait.

A first visit: follow feedback

  1. Open Explore and search for 'feedback'. Choose the entry matching your question.
  2. Read the short explanation and its status. Follow a source if you need the author's fuller argument.
  3. Open a connection. Read what it says before assuming it means influence or agreement.
  4. Choose the map to see neighbouring ideas. Start with one or two steps from the entry; use filters to show ideas, people, practice or disagreement.
  5. Follow one useful link, or return to the entry. A shared map URL retains the selected focus and filters.

Choose between the views

Explore is the searchable list. Guided journeys explain a sequence of ideas. Map lets you inspect recorded connections; on a small screen, card view provides a readable alternative. Ask helps locate relevant entries and prepares source context for inspection; it doesn’t send your question to an AI service automatically.

Read the map without overreading it

Select an entry to highlight its connections. Select a line to inspect its exact relationship and evidence. Zoom or switch to cards when labels become crowded. Use 'Everything' for the complete recorded graph; the other layers show selected kinds of connection.

Position, size and density are features of this representation. They don't establish intellectual importance, historical priority or agreement. The number of links reflects what's recorded here.

Practice and further reading

The practice collection provides exercises and worked cases with stated limits. Learning and training links to courses and provider resources. Design Horizon selections offer another way into design and systems change.

When something looks wrong

Use Contribute. Name the entry or connection, explain the change and give a source or passage. The form prepares an email or GitHub issue; you decide whether to send it. A submitted item enters review, not automatic publication.
