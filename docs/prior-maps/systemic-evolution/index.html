What the source actually says

The URANOS page says that nodes denote topics; scientific work or research areas; and that directed edges illustrate major influences between topics. Its published colour legend classifies scientific realms. The GraphML carries no edge-specific label, citation or finer influence type, so that generic statement is the limit of what we import.

Read every line as “the source map reports a major influence”, not “The Necessary Tangle has verified this influence”. Colour identifies a realm; it is not evidence and does not tell us whether the influence was teaching, citation, derivation, collaboration or something else.

Files, changes and review

Provenance, permission and credit

The published history records the map as originated by Eric Schwarz in 1996; extended in 1998 with material from Will Durant’s The Story of Philosophy; elaborated in 2000-01 for the International Institute for General Systems Studies; and extended in 2016 by Benjamin Hadorn. In accordance with the permission response, the original material and extensions are attributed to Eric Schwarz, Benjamin Hadorn and Beat Hirsbrunner.

On 26 August 2026 Benjamin Hadorn authorised the requested retention, parsing, visualisation, annotation, extension, derivative publication and independently checked incorporation, subject to full attribution and pre-publication review of modifications. He confirmed that separate approaches to the other parties were not required for the complete version if those conditions were met. This project-specific permission is not presented as a general CC BY-SA relicensing of the original. On 31 August 2026, he confirmed that he had checked the exact checksummed package and approved use of the enhanced version in this way.

The map was already in Benjamin P Taylor’s 2019 SysCoI prior-map collection. Nigel Williams extracted and analysed the GraphML and built the first comparator implementation; Benjamin P Taylor supplied the prior registration, permission work and cumulative editorial reconciliation.
