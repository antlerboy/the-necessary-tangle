Homeostasis

The organised maintenance of internal variables within viable ranges despite disturbance; the relevant variables, ranges, mechanisms and time scales must be specified.

Review status

Summary

Homeostasis is dynamic maintenance, not stillness. A living system keeps selected internal variables within viable ranges while energy, matter and information continue to flow and conditions continue to change.

Implications for practice

The idea is useful for understanding stability and resistance to disturbance. In organisations it is often used metaphorically; the metaphor only earns its keep when the variables, ranges and mechanisms being maintained are named.

Key distinctions

  • homeostasis vs stasis
  • maintained condition vs one feedback loop
  • viable range vs exact set point
  • physiological concept vs organisational analogy

Historical development

  • Claude Bernard: internal milieu as precursor
  • Walter Cannon 1929: physiological homeostasis formulation
  • cybernetics connected homeostasis with regulation and feedback

Ideas it depends on

  • essential variables
  • viable ranges
  • disturbance
  • regulatory processes
  • energy and material exchange

Further developments

  • adaptive regulation
  • organisational homeostasis metaphors
  • resilience and viability debates

Connections to practice

  • ask what the system is working to keep unchanged
  • distinguish survival from flourishing
  • look for hidden work that maintains dysfunction
  • identify who benefits from the maintained state

Common confusions

  • nothing changes
  • homeostasis is always desirable
  • all resistance is irrational
  • feedback and homeostasis are synonyms

Further checks

  • Bernard primary lineage
  • allostasis and adaptive ranges
  • responsible use in social systems
