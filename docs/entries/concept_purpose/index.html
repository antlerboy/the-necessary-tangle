Purpose

A context-dependent orientation or attribution that makes some outcomes, distinctions and actions relevant and supplies criteria for judging performance or viability.

Review status

Profile: research pass needs editor. Entry: research pass needs editor.

Summary

Purpose may be declared, negotiated, inferred from behaviour or attributed by an observer. These forms can conflict. Systems practice needs the conflict visible rather than forcing one slogan to do all the work.

Implications for practice

Purpose shapes boundaries, measures and acceptable trade-offs. A system can perform efficiently against an espoused purpose while repeatedly producing something else.

Key distinctions

  • espoused purpose vs observed effects
  • purpose of an actor vs attributed purpose of a system
  • purpose vs goal
  • plural purposes vs one mission statement

Historical development

  • teleology and purposive behaviour
  • cybernetics
  • soft systems and purposeful activity
  • management practice

Ideas it depends on

  • observer or participant
  • context
  • valued differences
  • criteria of success

Further developments

  • boundary
  • measurement
  • regulation
  • accountability
  • ethics

Connections to practice

  • name whose purpose is meant
  • compare intent with repeated effects
  • look for incompatible purposes
  • avoid measuring proxies as if they were purposes

Common confusions

  • systems have one objective purpose
  • the stated mission describes what the system does
  • purpose eliminates conflict
  • purpose is merely a motivational slogan

Further checks

  • Rosenblueth-Wiener-Bigelow

  • ethics of attributed purpose
