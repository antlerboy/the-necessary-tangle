Distinction

A situated act of marking a difference so that one side can be indicated, discussed or acted upon; the distinction reveals some possibilities and leaves others unmarked.

Summary

Distinctions make a world manageable enough for thought and action. They do not merely copy divisions already waiting in reality; they select, stabilise and test differences for a purpose.

Implications for practice

Every system map, category, KPI, service boundary and diagnosis begins with distinctions. Hidden distinctions are often more influential than explicit conclusions.

Key distinctions

  • distinction vs classification
  • marked side vs unmarked remainder
  • useful distinction vs true-for-all-time division
  • difference noticed vs difference made consequential

Historical development

  • logic and mathematics of distinction
  • cybernetic observer-inclusion
  • constructivist and enactive epistemology
  • systems practice and boundary critique

Ideas it depends on

  • an observer or system capable of indication
  • context and purpose
  • contrast or difference

Further developments

  • boundary

Connections to practice

  • ask what difference is being marked
  • name what falls into the unmarked remainder
  • test whether another distinction changes action
  • avoid turning a convenient cut into a natural kind

Common confusions

  • distinctions are arbitrary
  • distinctions are simply discovered
  • one correct decomposition exists
  • drawing more categories necessarily increases understanding
