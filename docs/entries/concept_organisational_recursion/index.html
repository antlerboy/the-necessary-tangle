Organisational recursion

A domain-specific use of recursion in which an organisational system contains operational units that may themselves be treated as systems with structurally corresponding viability requirements at another level.

Summary

In the VSM tradition, a viable operational unit may itself contain operations and meta-systemic functions. The same broad viability requirements recur, while purpose, environment, scale and implementation differ at each level.

Implications for practice

Organisations often centralise decisions that require local variety or mistake reporting lines for system levels. Recursion helps locate the system-in-focus and test whether each operational unit has sufficient autonomy and regulatory support.

Key distinctions

  • recursive viability vs repeated organisation chart
  • system-in-focus vs fixed hierarchy level
  • structural correspondence vs identical implementation
  • autonomy vs independence
  • recursion vs decomposition

Historical development

  • Stafford Beer developed recursive viability within management cybernetics
  • Diagnosing the System for Organizations made the model operational
  • later VSM practice developed recursive mapping techniques

Ideas it depends on

  • recursion

  • operational autonomy

Further developments

  • recursive VSM diagnosis
  • multi-level governance
  • autonomy-cohesion design
  • escalation and exception principles

Connections to practice

  • choose the system-in-focus before mapping
  • test whether operational units are genuinely viable or merely departments
  • map environments at each level
  • avoid forcing one diagram onto every scale

Common confusions

  • every hierarchy is recursive
  • recursion means the same five departments at every level
  • autonomy means no coordination
  • one VSM diagram represents the whole organisation once and for all
