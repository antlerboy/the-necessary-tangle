Recursion

A family of domain-specific relations in which a definition, operation, structure or organisation is reapplied to instances of the same kind. A conforming account must state the domain, recurrence rule and boundary or termination condition; organisational recursion is not automatically computational recursion or visual self-similarity.

Summary

Recursion is a family resemblance across domains, not one universal mechanism. A definition may refer to itself through smaller cases; a computation may call itself; an organisation may contain operational systems with corresponding viability requirements.

Implications for practice

Nested diagrams easily look profound. A sound recursive claim states what recurs, what changes between levels, what the base or boundary condition is and why the same structural relation applies.

Key distinctions

  • recursive definition vs recursive computation
  • recursion vs iteration
  • recursion vs hierarchy
  • recursion vs fractal self-similarity
  • organisational recursion vs mathematical recursion
  • recursion vs reflexivity

Historical development

  • formal recursive definition and recursive-function theory
  • computability in the 1930s
  • linguistic self-embedding
  • Beer and organisational recursion
  • second-order observation and reflexivity as a distinct neighbouring lineage

Ideas it depends on

  • identity of the operation or structure
  • rule for moving to another instance or level
  • base case or boundary condition
  • domain in which sameness is claimed

Further developments

  • recursive functions
  • self-embedding grammars
  • nested viable systems
  • some fractal constructions
  • some forms of self-reference

Connections to practice

  • name the system-in-focus
  • show what recurs and what does not
  • state stopping and boundary conditions
  • do not infer sameness from visual nesting

Common confusions

  • repetition is recursion
  • hierarchy and recursion are synonyms
  • fractal means recursive and vice versa
  • Turing invented recursion
  • nested boxes prove organisational recursion
