Systems Sciences: a reading route

Systems Sciences brings questions about wholes, relationships and organisation into conversation across disciplines. This route starts with an institutional account of that purpose, then offers two different ways into the literature: a reference work and a programme of critical systems practice.

What crosses disciplinary boundaries?

The International Society for the Systems Sciences describes a programme of interdisciplinary inquiry: looking for comparable concepts and models, testing useful transfers between fields, developing theory where it is missing, reducing duplicated effort and improving communication among specialists. Its account also includes the practical use of systems methodologies.

This is the ISSS account of its purpose, not a single definition binding every systems tradition. Similarity between two models is a question to investigate; it does not by itself show that the models are interchangeable.

A reference route: François’s encyclopedia

The International Encyclopedia of Systems and Cybernetics, edited by Charles François, offers a reference route into the field’s vocabulary. The Bertalanffy Center identifies the first edition as 1997 and the basis of the online version as the second edition of 2004. It records François’s 2018 wish to transfer stewardship to the Center.

Use the encyclopedia to locate a term, follow its references and compare meanings across traditions. An entry’s inclusion is not evidence that every school uses the term in the same way.

Michael C. Jackson: choosing and combining approaches

Jackson’s Critical Systems Thinking and the Management of Complexity examines systems approaches and their application to complex interventions. Creative Problem Solving: Total Systems Intervention, co-authored with Robert L. Flood, addresses how different systems methodologies can be used in a complementary way.

Read these as routes into methodological choice: what does an approach make visible, what does it leave out, and what changes when it is used alongside another? These questions are an editorial reading suggestion, rather than a claim that all systems approaches can be combined without difficulty.
