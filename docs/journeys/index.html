Choose a reading journey

The journeys connect several entries around a question. Each entry links to its sources and records its review status.

  • Emergence without magic

    A route through local interaction, cross-scale pattern and the limits of command.

  • From feedback to regulation

    A guided route through feedback, feedforward, homeostasis and regulatory capacity.

  • From variety to viable organisation

    A practical route from distinctions and disturbance to the Viable System Model.

  • How a system becomes visible

    A route through the usually hidden work that makes a situation intelligible enough for action.

  • How a whole persists without becoming rigid

    A route through the central tension of viable organisation: changing enough without dissolving the whole.

  • How to read The Necessary Tangle

    A short orientation to typed relations, evidence and disagreement.

  • Recursion: more than nested boxes

    A route that separates formal recursion from hierarchy, fractals and organisational analogy.

  • What happens at the boundary

    A practical route through the transformations hidden inside hand-offs, reporting and policy-to-practice translation.

  • How ideas travel through people

    A route through the human lineage layer without turning every contact into influence.

  • Power enters before the intervention

    A route showing why power is built into systems practice before anyone chooses a method.

  • How a collection becomes a field — or pretends to

    A route through the difference between inventorying a collection and understanding a field.

  • Social complexity without the control-room fantasy

    A route from complexity science to a radically social account of organising and practice.

  • Six systems things which are not the same thing

    A short route through six terms which are routinely collapsed into one agreeable blur.

  • Viability, balance, semantics and strategy

    Connects Ivo Velitchkov's work on viability and organisational balance with Patrick Hoverstadt's management cybernetics, systems laws and strategy practice.

  • Inquiry, governance and intervention

    Moves from inquiry into contested situations through explicit boundary judgement and organisational design to systemic governance, intervention and whole-system conduct.

  • Snowden, Cynefin and the jobs sources can do

    Starts with Dave Snowden's body of work, separates its people, frameworks, tools and publications, and shows why canonical first-party sources are indispensable but not self-validating.

  • David Ing: systems in plural

    Move through David Ing’s own systems work and the documentary practices that make systems lineages inspectable.

  • Core systems practice: concepts, approaches, models and intervention

    A practice route through the competence spine behind professional systems work, with the four widely named approaches kept in relation rather than treated as rival brands.

  • The Grammar is a web, not a list

    Use eight laws and principles to move through all nine Grammar thinking patterns and into the wider evidence graph.

  • From midge swarms to municipal practice

    Follow Damian Allen's self-described lineage into Doncaster Thrive, then test where public sources, practice claims and unresolved theory diverge.

  • Who gets to count as a systems thinker?

    Move from a useful biographical canon through its boundary choices, epistemic closure and exclusion, critical pluralism, decolonial questions and evidence-led lineage recovery.

  • Reading cybernetics as it was taking shape

    Follow the early lists into communication, adaptation, and social purpose, with a visible boundary between cited works and inspected passages.

  • The message arrived. What happened?

    A practical route through communication questions, feedback, and response capacity. The links between the steps are editorial questions, not assertions of historical influence.

  • How a reading list makes a field

    Read the bibliography as evidence of choices about a field, while keeping historical claims open to verification.

  • Which distinctions can this system act on?

    Trace what must survive between a disturbance and an effective response.

  • What changed when we learned?

    Distinguish a changed response from changes to the premises through which responses are chosen.

  • What happens at a boundary?

    Compare four accounts of boundaries without collapsing their different questions.

  • From observation to an action someone can take

    Follow different accounts of a situation through the question of who can judge, challenge, and act. This is an editorial sequence, not a historical lineage.

  • What is producing the pattern?

    Compare spatial instability, temporal sensitivity, movement feedback, and repeated-game cooperation by asking what each model actually assumes and explains. This is an editorial sequence, not a historical lineage or a common organisational law.

  • What does a knowledge web commit us to?

    An editorial route through seven existing records, asking what was proposed, what was implemented, whose meanings are retained, and which values guide the programme. It asserts no historical influence.
