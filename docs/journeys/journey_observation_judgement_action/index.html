From observation to an action someone can take

Situated accounts, boundary judgement, methods, and authority

Follow different accounts of a situation through the question of who can judge, challenge, and act. This is an editorial sequence, not a historical lineage.

This is an editorial reading route. Claims about influence or dependence need the evidence recorded against the relevant connection.

Follow this journey interactively

  1. Name the distinction

    What has to be selected for this observation to exist? State the category, the excluded population, and what would make you revise the account. Observer-dependence doesn't make evidence irrelevant.

    Read Observer

  2. Keep differences available

    What changes when another account enters the inquiry? Distinguish new evidence, a different purpose, and a disagreement about values. More perspectives don't imply consensus.

    Read Multiple perspectives

  3. Make the judgement contestable

    Who defines improvement, controls resources, supplies expertise, and can challenge what counts? Keep those affected visible even when they aren't among those deciding.

    Read Boundary critique

  4. Try the boundary dispute

    Use the constructed case to compare actual and ought accounts. Record unresolved disagreement and what the inquiry can inform without claiming authority to settle the dispute.

    Read Practice: Critical Systems Heuristics: inspect all twelve boundary categories

  5. Trace conditions for action

    Which existing rules, resources, relationships, and practices enable or constrain the proposed action? Ask how the action might reproduce or change those conditions rather than explain everything through an isolated choice.

    Read Structure and agency

  6. Justify the next method

    Carry the question, evidence, participants' meanings, and unresolved disagreement across the transition. Name the authority for the next action and a reason to revise or stop. The sequence connects questions without making the underlying theories interchangeable.

    Read Practice: Multi-methodology: justify the move from one method to another
