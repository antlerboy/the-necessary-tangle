Readable atlas · Release 0.37

Viability, balance, semantics and strategy

A route through viable organisation, requisite inefficiency, explicit meaning and relational strategy.

Connects Ivo Velitchkov's work on viability and organisational balance with Patrick Hoverstadt's management cybernetics, systems laws and strategy practice.

This is an editorial reading route. Claims about influence or dependence need the evidence recorded against the relevant connection.

Follow this journey interactively

  1. Viable is not simply fittest

    Viability concerns continuing as a recognisable whole under change. It is not automatically the same as comparative evolutionary fitness, short-term survival or maximum efficiency.

    Read Viability

  2. A specific evolutionary challenge

    Maturana and Mpodozis propose natural drift as the generative process and selection as a consequence. The atlas includes this as a contested theory, not as biological consensus.

    Read Natural drift

  3. Viability, balance and explicit meaning

    Velitchkov's work connects viable organisation to Essential Balances, requisite inefficiency and explicit semantic graphs.

    Read Ivo Velitchkov

  4. Keep tensions alive

    Autonomy and cohesion, stability and diversity, exploration and exploitation are not dilemmas to eliminate but conditions to keep sensing and adjusting.

    Read Essential Balances

  5. Do not optimise away the response

    Slack and redundancy can carry the unused variety that becomes essential when conditions change.

    Read Requisite inefficiency

  6. Say what the line means

    A graph should expose entity type, relation type, direction, evidence, status and scope. Visual closeness is not enough.

    Read Explicit semantics

  7. A live semantic comparator

    Nodica provides an RDF-based graph visualisation comparator. The atlas remains a different project but now documents the comparison and its own semantic contract.

    Read Nodica

  8. Management cybernetics in practice

    Hoverstadt's work connects VSM, systems laws, organisation design, strategy and transformation.

    Read Patrick Hoverstadt

  9. Patterns and laws

    The Grammar distinguishes thinking patterns from laws and principles, offering a structured route into systemic reasoning.

    Read The Grammar of Systems II

  10. Strategy in relationship

    Patterns of Strategy models strategic fit, power, time and response across an ecosystem rather than treating strategy as an internal plan.

    Read Patterns of Strategy
