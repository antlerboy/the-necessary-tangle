How a system becomes visible

Observer, distinction, boundary, purpose and information

A route through the usually hidden work that makes a situation intelligible enough for action.

This is an editorial reading route. Claims about influence or dependence need the evidence recorded against the relevant connection.

Follow this journey interactively

  1. Locate the observer

    Every account begins from capacities, sensors, histories and stakes. Naming the observer makes limits and responsibilities inspectable.

    Read Observer

  2. Notice the cut

    The observer marks differences. The cut makes some patterns graspable and leaves others in the unmarked remainder.

    Read Distinction

  3. Form a system of concern

    A cluster of distinctions becomes a boundary around what counts, who counts and where responsibility is placed.

    Read Boundary

  4. Ask what the boundary is for

    Different purposes create different relevant systems. Purpose also supplies the criteria by which performance and viability will be judged.

    Read Purpose

  5. See what can become signal

    The boundary, code and receiving organisation decide which differences become information and which become noise.

    Read Information
