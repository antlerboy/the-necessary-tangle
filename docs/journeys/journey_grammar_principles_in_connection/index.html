The Grammar is a web, not a list

Use eight laws and principles to move through all nine Grammar thinking patterns and into the wider evidence graph.

This is an editorial reading route. Claims about influence or dependence need the evidence recorded against the relevant connection.

Follow this journey interactively

  1. A system begins with a distinction

    Calling something a system draws a boundary and exposes the observer's choice. Boundaries are made for a purpose, not merely found.

    Read Law of Calling

  2. Persistence requires noticing difference

    A system is recognisable through patterns which persist across observations, even while its parts and activity change.

    Read System Stability Principle

  3. Connections multiply faster than elements

    Relating creates possibility and structural complexity. Counting parts alone hides the combinatorial burden of their possible relationships.

    Read Network Power Law

  4. Loops can dominate inputs

    Behaviour may arise more from feedback structure than from the initial shove. This is why changing inputs can leave the pattern intact.

    Read Feedback Dominance Theorem

  5. Whole-level properties need organisation

    Emergence is not magic and not a synonym for self-organisation. It directs attention to interaction, level and pattern.

    Read Principle of Emergence

  6. The whole persists through balances

    Autonomy and cohesion, stability and adaptation, present operation and future change have to be held together rather than optimised separately.

    Read Viability Principle

  7. Models are selective

    Incomplete knowledge is not an excuse to stop. It is a reason to state boundaries, uncertainty and the purpose of the model.

    Read Darkness Principle

  8. Action returns through relationship

    In a connected system, action on another changes the conditions of the actor. The map closes by returning relating to loops.

    Read Law of Reciprocity of Connections
