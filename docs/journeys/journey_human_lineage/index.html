How ideas travel through people

Teaching, collaboration, institutions and influence are different lines

A route through the human lineage layer without turning every contact into influence.

This is an editorial reading route. Claims about influence or dependence need the evidence recorded against the relevant connection.

Follow this journey interactively

  1. Begin with a named contributor

    A person's work can be historically central without making that person the sole origin of a field.

    Read Norbert Wiener

  2. Separate parallel development

    Shared problems and correspondence can coexist with independent lines of work. The evidence must say which relation is being claimed.

    Read W. Ross Ashby

  3. Add institutions and gatherings

    Laboratories, conferences and editorial settings often carry ideas between people more accurately than a chain of person-to-person arrows.

    Read Heinz von Foerster

  4. Trace adoption and transformation

    A practitioner can inherit concepts, reshape them and build methods whose practical lineage differs from their formal antecedents.

    Read Stafford Beer

  5. End at a method, not a family myth

    The method gathers formal, historical, organisational and practical strands. The atlas should show those different strands rather than compress them into one heroic genealogy.

    Read Viable System Model (VSM)
