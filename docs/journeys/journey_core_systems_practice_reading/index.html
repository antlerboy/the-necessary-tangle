Core systems practice: concepts, approaches, models and intervention

A practice route through the competence spine behind professional systems work, with the four widely named approaches kept in relation rather than treated as rival brands.

This is an editorial reading route. Claims about influence or dependence need the evidence recorded against the relevant connection.

Follow this journey interactively

  1. Start with practice, not a favourite method

    Systems practice joins concepts, judgement, modelling, engagement, intervention and learning.

    Read Core systems practice spine

  2. Use concepts and laws as working constraints

    Feedback, variety, emergence, boundaries and related concepts inform method use without replacing situated judgement.

    Read Systems laws

  3. Interrogate boundaries and legitimacy

    CSH keeps boundary judgements, affected parties, expertise and legitimacy explicit.

    Read Critical Systems Heuristics (CSH)

  4. Model purposeful activity and worldviews

    SSM organises inquiry into problematic situations without pretending the conceptual model is the real world.

    Read Soft Systems Methodology (SSM)

  5. Model feedback, stocks, flows and delay

    System Dynamics makes dynamic hypotheses testable and forces attention to behaviour over time.

    Read System Dynamics

  6. Model viability and recursive organisation

    VSM asks how organisations handle variety, autonomy, cohesion, intelligence and policy across recursive levels.

    Read Viable System Model (VSM)

  7. Combine approaches deliberately

    Professional practice requires understanding strengths, limits and complementarities rather than method loyalty.

    Read approach_family_multi_methodology_including_sosm

  8. Recover the histories behind the toolbox

    The reading list should reconnect methods to the thinkers, problems and traditions from which they emerged.

    Read Systems Thinkers

  9. Keep epistemology alive

    Bateson keeps relation, learning and the ecology of ideas in view.

    Read Steps to an Ecology of Mind

  10. Put the observer back in

    Von Foerster makes reflexivity and responsibility unavoidable.

    Read Understanding Understanding

  11. Remember the intervention relationship

    Even good systems models fail when contracting, ownership and engagement fail.

    Read Flawless Consulting: A Guide to Getting Your Expertise Used
