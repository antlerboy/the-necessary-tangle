## Immediate provocation

David Ing supplied the formulation Benjamin records as the immediate provocation for the human-lineage layer:

> ‘We need to map the constellation of influences around practitioners.’

That sentence changes the object. It points away from a neat succession of schools and towards the actual, overlapping routes through which people encounter, teach, combine, dispute and use ideas. David also helped preserve and support the public SysCoI and model.report lineage.

## Earlier bodies of knowledge and practice frameworks

The project develops the connected approach of the original SCiO Systems Thinking Body of Knowledge and later competency work. Particular acknowledgement is due to Tony Korycki and other contributors to the earlier SysBoK material, and to the wider SCiO community whose practice, teaching and argument continue to expose gaps and category mistakes.

Patrick Hoverstadt, Lucy Loh and colleagues associated with the Grammar of Systems supplied an important named corpus of laws and principles which the atlas treats as inspectable source material rather than as unquestionable doctrine.

## Comparators, maps and archives

Igor Perko's researchers-network work provides a major comparator for the ‘rock family tree’ ambition. Brian Castellani's maps of the complexity sciences and the work of many other map makers provide both rich source material and design challenges. The point of criticism is not to pretend that earlier maps were worthless, but to make their purposes, categories, boundary choices and kinds of line explicit.

The Systems Community of Inquiry, the preserved model.report archive, Monoskop, professional bodies, public archives, libraries and many individual scholars make a large part of this work possible.
