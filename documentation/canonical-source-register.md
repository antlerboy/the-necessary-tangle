# Canonical-source register: first pass

A canonical source is a preferred route for a particular evidential job. It is not a universal authority and it does not make every statement found near it true.

The register separates source roles so that a finding aid is not treated as a primary text, a professional bibliography is not treated as proof of influence, and a project history is not mistaken for independent evaluation.

## Source tiers

### Primary works and archives

These are original texts, correspondence, notes, recordings or datasets, or institutional repositories holding them. They support the strongest historical and conceptual statements when cited with exact item and locator details.

### Institutional finding aids and archive gateways

These establish holdings, provenance, dates and routes to records. They do not by themselves support detailed intellectual claims about the contents.

### Professional-body archives and histories

These are strong sources for organisational chronology, events, publications and declared positions. They may reflect the body's own memory and boundary choices.

### Field bibliographies and curated corpora

These are valuable for discovery, coverage checking and identifying canonical debates. Selection into a bibliography or anthology is not evidence of correctness, influence or completeness.

### Discovery and reference corpora

These help find terms, people and sources. Their definitions and histories need to be traced to named authors and stronger records before they carry substantial claims.

## Registered first-pass sources

### W. Ross Ashby Digital Archive

Role: primary archive.

Use for Ashby's journals, notebooks, index cards, chronology and intellectual development. Cite a volume, page or card rather than the archive landing page wherever possible.

### MIT Norbert Wiener papers, 1898–1966

Role: institutional primary archive.

Use for correspondence, manuscripts and chronology. The collection description establishes scope; individual records support particular statements.

### University of Illinois von Foerster and Biological Computer Laboratory gateway

Role: institutional archive gateway.

Use to locate BCL publications, contracts, conference records and von Foerster papers. Follow the finding aid to item-level evidence.

### LJMU Falcondale Collection

Role: primary audiovisual archive.

Use the Stafford Beer recordings and transcripts for his own explanations of variety, viability, the Viable System Model and Syntegration. Cite the tape and transcript location.

### Gregory Bateson papers finding aid

Role: institutional finding aid.

Use to establish archival holdings and provenance. Do not use the finding-aid description as a substitute for the correspondence or manuscript itself.

### American Society for Cybernetics archives

Role: professional-body archive and discovery source.

Use for society chronology, events, publications and declared positions. Test wider historical statements against independent and primary records.

### System Dynamics bibliography

Role: field bibliography.

Use for systematic discovery and coverage checking. Inclusion does not establish influence, quality or consensus.

### Santa Fe Institute Foundational Papers in Complexity Science project

Role: curated corpus description.

Use the official project page to establish the collection's purpose and scope. Record each selected paper and each editorial introduction separately.

### Principia Cybernetica Web

Role: historical connected hypertext, primary project record and discovery corpus.

Use project-authored pages for the project's own concepts, history and design claims. Mark incomplete nodes as incomplete. Trace claims about external thinkers to their primary works. Do not treat the whole hypertext as one consistent authority.

## Source-use rules

For every substantial statement, record:

- the source role;
- the exact item;
- the locator;
- whether the wording is quoted, summarised or inferred;
- whose account it is;
- whether counter-evidence or a rival account is known;
- the public-link status;
- the curatorial status.

A source can be canonical for discovery and weak for adjudication. The evidence record, not the prestige of the host institution, carries the particular statement.
