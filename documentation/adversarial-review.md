# Adversarial relationship and experience review

Date: 2026-08-14  
Pass: 6

## Test used

For each exact source/type/target duplicate, unlocated legacy candidate and maintained inferred edge, the review asked:

1. Does the locator establish this particular typed and directed claim?
2. Does the evidence establish the public phrase, including frequency words such as “often”?
3. Is a stronger version of the same route already maintained?
4. Would a reader be misled by retaining the edge merely as provisional?
5. Can distinct evidence be merged without losing source or work locators?

The answer “interesting and plausible” was not treated as evidence.

## Duplicate decisions

The baseline contained 21 exact source/type/target duplicate groups.

- Seventeen unlocated bibliographic duplicates were removed; all nineteen retained authorship records for the affected works were given volume, item and title locators.
- Two generic IEEE source-navigation edges were removed because claim-located edges already connect the same records.
- One unlocated Maxwell candidate was removed because a primary, page-located governor edge already exists.
- Two Rosenblueth–Wiener co-authorship edges represented two genuinely different works. They were merged into one edge whose wording and locator retain both works.

Result: zero exact duplicate triples.

## Unsupported candidate decisions

Twenty-five legacy candidates had no claim-level locator. They were not made stronger by a confidence number or a broad internal topic map. The pass retired them from the public graph, including:

- reversed feedback-to-cybernetics/control-theory directions already contradicted by stronger maintained edges;
- feedback-as-parent claims for double bind and single-/double-loop learning;
- a broad feedback-applies-quality-management claim;
- fourteen name-to-recursion lineage claims whose notes supplied relevance or resemblance but not located historical evidence.

Five inferred edges were also retired. Three inferred a whole-corpus “complements” relation from a framework grouping; two used definition comparisons to claim concepts are “often confused”, although the sources do not evidence frequency of confusion.

The von Domarus → McCulloch influence attribution remains as a visibly provisional candidate because it has a public secondary source and explicit claim record. It was not promoted: primary lineage evidence is still missing.

## Experience and accessibility review

The product review covers desktop and mobile routes, horizontal overflow, keyboard access to skip navigation, map nodes and edges, drawer dismissal, accessible names, duplicate DOM IDs, heading landmarks, focus visibility, light/dark presentation and the fixed Open updates control. Results and screenshots are recorded in the overnight log.

## Limits

This pass adversarially reviewed the weakest explicit classes and every exact duplicate; it did not reread all 1,670 remaining edges from first principles. Many interpreted crosswalks still cite broad internal registers. Removing weak edges also reduces some historical reach and can increase the thin cohort; that is preferable to preserving unsupported lineage.
