# The Complexity Podcast corpus

## Scope

Release 0.19 registers the complete public run of *COMPLEXITY*, the official podcast of the Santa Fe Institute, as a bounded source corpus.

The current RSS-mediated inventory contains 119 items published from 2019 to 2024. The Santa Fe Institute's official archive states that full transcripts for every episode are available through Simplecast.

This is a corpus intake, not a claim that 119 episodes have each received the same depth of interpretation.

## Canonical public sources

- Santa Fe Institute podcast archive: <https://web-prod.santafe.edu/culture/podcast-archive>
- Simplecast series and transcripts: <https://complexity.simplecast.com/>
- Official RSS feed: <https://feeds.simplecast.com/OzDH_At2>

## Source roles

### RSS feed

Use the feed for inventory, title, date, episode description and canonical episode URL. It does not by itself establish the truth of claims made in an episode.

### Episode transcript

Use the transcript to establish that a named participant said something at a particular point in a particular conversation. Transcripts may be machine-generated and edited. Important wording should be checked against the audio where exactness matters.

### Show notes

Use show notes to discover papers, books, people and related episodes. Follow the link and inspect the underlying source before using it to support a stronger scholarly or historical claim.

### Institutional publication

SFI's publication of the podcast establishes institutional selection and public circulation. A guest appearance does not prove agreement, priority, truth, field-wide importance or influence.

## Intake method

For each bounded thematic pass:

1. start from an existing atlas gap or a clear question;
2. inventory relevant episodes through the RSS feed;
3. read the transcript and retain a stable episode URL plus timestamp or transcript locator;
4. separate the guest's statement from the host's framing and the show-note summary;
5. check cited primary works where the proposed graph claim goes beyond ‘said in this episode’;
6. add only typed, warranted relations;
7. record disagreement, ambiguity and source limits beside the claim.

## Initial high-relevance route

The first review route prioritises episodes which bear directly on existing atlas concerns: context and explanation; distributed intelligence and collective computation; multiple perspectives; emergence and political economy; communication and information; power, justice and institutional knowledge; the physics and organisation of life; and the limits of machine intelligence.

This route is deliberately question-led. The alternative would be 119 shallow summaries and a handsome new fog bank.
