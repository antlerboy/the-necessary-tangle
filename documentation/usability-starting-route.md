# A calmer starting route

The full public graph is valuable, but it is a poor first sentence. New readers should not have to decode hundreds of nodes, several relation families and evidence states before they know what the atlas is for.

The public starting route should therefore offer three levels:

1. **Start with a question** — choose a small guided route such as viability, systems practice, human lineages or power and boundaries.
2. **See the neighbourhood** — open the selected entry with its immediate typed connections and a plain explanation of what the lines mean.
3. **Open the whole tangle** — retain the full public map for readers who want the complete graph, provenance layers and path finding.

This is not a simplified ‘beginner ontology’. It is progressive disclosure over the same evidence graph. The starting route should preserve the ability to inspect sources, uncertainty and rival accounts rather than replacing the graph with a polished story.

Ivo Velitchkov’s observation that the atlas was interesting but ‘scary’ is treated as a usability finding: richness without bearings becomes an exclusion mechanism.
