Black box: primary-source evidence note

Status: bounded research candidate, not applied or published; human editorial acceptance pending. Source: W. Ross Ashby, An Introduction to Cybernetics, 1956, 1957 second impression, chapter 6, https://ashby.info/Ashby-Introduction-to-Cybernetics.pdf. The archive provides two printed pages per PDF leaf; section numbers and printed pages below identify the relevant passage. The candidate links and paraphrases the work; no source pages are reproduced.

F1. Ashby provisionally specifies the ways the investigator can act on the box and observe it. His basic record is a time-ordered protocol of input and output states. The selected input and output, and the sequence of observations, matter to what can be inferred. §§6/2–6/3, printed pp 87–89, PDF leaves 49–50.

F2. Ashby sorts the observed transitions to look for a single-valued representation, explicitly noting an unobserved transition in his example. If observed transitions are not single-valued, he considers changing the variables recorded or seeking statistical regularity. His representation is a description of the selected observations; a finite consistent record does not by itself establish that every untested state or condition will follow it. §§6/3, 6/5–6/6, printed pp 89–92, PDF leaves 50–52. The final sentence is an editorial qualification of evidence from finite tests, not a quotation or a claim that Ashby denies the usefulness of representations.

F3. Ashby says some states cannot be revisited at will, limiting repeated examination. He also says that, where a determinate representation is obtained, some immediate-effect relations can be deduced, while different internal arrangements can realise the same observed relation. He illustrates the latter with alternative switching networks; the behaviour does not uniquely specify their connections. §§6/6–6/7, printed pp 92–93, PDF leaf 52, Figure 6/7/1. This permits scoped deductions; it is not a claim that behaviour reveals nothing about mechanisms or that all conceivable internal structures fit.

F4. Ashby presents a mechanical system and an electrical system whose chosen input-output relations match under the stipulated conditions, despite different physical constructions. He cautions that a correspondence may hold only over a range. This ideal comparison should not be conflated with two systems that merely agree on a finite set of trials. §6/8, printed pp 94–97, PDF leaves 53–54, Figure 6/8/1.

F5. Ashby shows that a partial description may be complete within the distinctions retained, and that a suitable grouping can preserve a determinate transformation. He also gives a grouping that does not. This is more specific than saying that any convenient simplification is valid. §6/13, printed pp 103–105, PDF leaves 57–58.

Editorial illustration: the reader's two candidate input-output rules are stipulated for three inputs and agree on the two inputs tried. Their disagreement on the third shows underdetermination by that finite record only. It makes no claim about their internal construction, all possible histories, organisations, or performance.
