Reading the CoExplorer maps

19 September 2026 · Release 0.25

A map can show an argument, a classification, a history, or a proposed mechanism. The meaning of its lines determines what can be learned from it. These three CoExplorer maps offer useful comparisons with The Necessary Tangle; the two Anselm reading lists remain registered for reading.

Three maps, with different questions

The cybernetics map organises concepts around named thinkers. The systems map presents knowledge traditions and a genealogy. The Troncale map focuses on systems concepts and linkage propositions. They are complementary reading entrances, not interchangeable histories of one settled field.

Peter Tuddenham's CoExplorer teaching project, credited with the College of Exploration, is a key comparison source. Credit for the project does not make every interpretation an established historical or scientific claim. This page links to the original work and retains its separate identity.

The maps' displayed totals disagree across pages and sometimes within a page. The source register below records the observed numbers. No complete dataset has been imported, and none of those totals is counted as verified Tangle coverage.

Reading a relationship

The CoExplorer guide makes the kind of relation visible. The comparison below is Tangle's editorial assessment of how to preserve those distinctions. It is not an automatic translation of one graph into the other.

A claim follows necessarilyA definitional prerequisite can identify what a concept presupposes.A prerequisite and a consequence point in different directions. State the claim in a sentence before choosing an arrow.

These distinctions can be expressed through the atlas's existing statement fields. This review adds no relationships from the external maps to the atlas. A future import would need to resolve identities and verify each claim's meaning and source, rather than convert every line by matching its label.

A proposition has a range of validity

McNamara and Troncale's 2012 paper, pp. 3–4, separates evidence for a relation in a particular system from the proposal that it applies elsewhere. Its criteria allow a directional or mutual operator between processes, with broader applicability requiring further investigation. This is a useful discipline for reading the later map.

For Tangle, the practical consequence is to keep three questions separate: what was observed, which relationship is proposed, and where that proposition has been tested. A statement about a physical system does not automatically become a warranted account of an organisation. This is an editorial application of the distinction, not an empirical finding from the paper.

The 1978 chapter's abstract and bibliographic record are available. The full chapter remains unread in this review. Its title and the later maps' counts cannot establish a reconciled list of original propositions.
